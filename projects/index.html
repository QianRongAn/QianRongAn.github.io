Equivariant Graph Neural Networks for TCR Off-Target Risk Prediction

Biomedical AI | Computational Immunology | Graph Deep Learning

Can molecular contacts help distinguish peptides that look structurally similar, but carry different risks of unintended TCR recognition?

My contributionIndependently led the project end to end, connecting structural feature engineering, equivariant graph modelling, ablation experiments, domain adaptation, and active-learning screening.

Goaladdress potential immunotherapy safety risks arising from T-cell receptor (TCR) cross-reactivity by building a molecular-structure-based compatibility scoring model that identifies off-target peptides at risk of unintended TCR recognition, and explore active-learning candidate screening strategies.

Feature engineering and ablationto separate structurally similar off-target peptides that traditional backbone-geometry features cannot distinguish, designed backbone versus side-chain contact ablation experiments; backbone coordinates alone yielded AUROC 0.500 on a grafted-decoy discrimination task, rising to 0.933 after adding side-chain contact features, confirming the decisive contribution of side-chain chemistry.

Generalization and domain adaptationto bridge the distribution gap between crystal-structure training data and predicted structures in application, introduced predicted-complex data for domain adaptation, raising cross-instance ranking concordance from 0.447 to 0.802.

Active learning and candidate screeningdesigned candidate sampling strategies based on expected information gain (EIG) and BALD, achieving approximately 2x the positive-recall gain of random-sampling and equivalent baselines at a 6% sampling budget in a simulated screen of about 4.65 million candidate peptides.

How the workflow fits togetherThe molecular structure provides the starting point. Backbone geometry and side-chain contacts form the structural representation used by the EGNN to score compatibility. Ablation experiments test which information drives discrimination; domain-adaptation experiments then examine transfer to predicted complexes. Active learning uses those scores and model uncertainty to prioritize the next candidates for screening.

Reading the resultsThe AUROC comparison concerns a specific grafted-decoy task, while ranking concordance measures a separate transfer experiment. The candidate-screening result comes from simulation. Together, these experiments examine representation, transfer, and sampling efficiency; they do not establish clinical safety or experimentally validated off-target recognition.

Tech stackPython, PyTorch, EGNN, NumPy, SciPy, scikit-learn, structural bioinformatics.

Evidence-Based Medicine Literature QA and Retraction-Risk Modelling

Medical NLP | Evidence Retrieval | Machine Learning

How can a medical answer show its sources, expose conflicting evidence, and make the limits of a risk prediction inspectable?

My contributionIndependently developed the literature-analysis tool and retraction-risk modelling workflow, spanning retrieval, evidence structuring, source traceability, model interpretation, and data-leakage auditing.

Goalfor clinical researchers and medical editors, build an evidence-analysis tool spanning medical-question retrieval, evidence grading, conclusion-conflict identification, and source traceability, together with a retraction-risk prediction model based on paper metadata.

Evidence retrieval and analysisbuilt a PubMed literature retrieval and question-answering pipeline on FastAPI using BM25 retrieval, PICO (population, intervention, comparator, outcome) question structuring, study-design identification, and five-level evidence grading; evidence disagreement is surfaced explicitly through support, oppose, and neutral conclusion classification with conflict detection, and conclusions in answers are traceable to specific PMIDs.

Retraction-risk predictionbuilt a retraction-risk dataset from 27,978 papers, modelled with LightGBM and interpreted with SHAP feature-contribution analysis; in the cross-validation experiments reported in the repository, LightGBM reached AUROC 0.856 and PR-AUC 0.812.

Data-quality and leakage auditidentified and handled publication-type label leakage and implicit leakage from cleared or replaced post-retraction abstracts, and assessed generalization through temporal extrapolation validation split by publication year; SHAP analysis revealed pronounced temporal censoring bias in publication year, avoiding mistaking superficial predictive performance for a genuine risk signal.

Engineering and validationimplemented online PubMed retrieval, offline corpus fallback, TTL caching, and flagging of retracted, questioned, or corrected literature; the project includes 30 unit tests, with repository-reported cache-hit latency of about 6 ms.

From question to evidencePICO structuring separates the population, intervention, comparator, and outcome before evidence is reviewed. Retrieved studies are organized by design and evidence level, and their conclusions are classified as supporting, opposing, or neutral. PMID links let readers return to the underlying paper and inspect disagreements rather than relying on a single unqualified answer.

Interpreting retraction riskThe metadata model is a separate analytical component from the question-answering pipeline. SHAP helps inspect which features contribute to its predictions. Leakage checks and publication-year splits address whether performance reflects information available at prediction time, rather than signals introduced after retraction. The reported cross-validation scores should be read alongside those audits, not as proof of prospective reliability.

Tech stackPython, FastAPI, BM25, PICO, LightGBM, SHAP, PubMed API.
