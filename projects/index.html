Equivariant Graph Neural Networks for TCR Off-Target Risk Prediction

Biomedical AI | Computational Immunology | Graph Deep Learning

Can molecular contacts help distinguish peptides that look structurally similar, but carry different risks of unintended TCR recognition?

My contributionIndependently led the project end to end, connecting structural feature engineering, equivariant graph modelling, ablation experiments, domain adaptation, and active-learning screening.

Goaladdress potential immunotherapy safety risks arising from T-cell receptor (TCR) cross-reactivity by building a molecular-structure-based compatibility scoring model that identifies off-target peptides at risk of unintended TCR recognition, and explore active-learning candidate screening strategies.

Feature engineering and ablationto separate structurally similar off-target peptides that traditional backbone-geometry features cannot distinguish, designed backbone versus side-chain contact ablation experiments; backbone coordinates alone yielded AUROC 0.500 on a grafted-decoy discrimination task, rising to 0.933 after adding side-chain contact features, confirming the decisive contribution of side-chain chemistry.

Generalization and domain adaptationto bridge the distribution gap between crystal-structure training data and predicted structures in application, introduced predicted-complex data for domain adaptation, raising cross-instance ranking concordance from 0.447 to 0.802.

Active learning and candidate screeningdesigned candidate sampling strategies based on expected information gain (EIG) and BALD, achieving approximately 2x the positive-recall gain of random-sampling and equivalent baselines at a 6% sampling budget in a simulated screen of about 4.65 million candidate peptides.

How the workflow fits togetherThe molecular structure provides the starting point. Backbone geometry and side-chain contacts form the structural representation used by the EGNN to score compatibility. Ablation experiments test which information drives discrimination; domain-adaptation experiments then examine transfer to predicted complexes. Active learning uses those scores and model uncertainty to prioritize the next candidates for screening.

Reading the resultsThe AUROC comparison concerns a specific grafted-decoy task, while ranking concordance measures a separate transfer experiment. The candidate-screening result comes from simulation. Together, these experiments examine representation, transfer, and sampling efficiency; they do not establish clinical safety or experimentally validated off-target recognition.

Tech stackPython, PyTorch, EGNN, NumPy, SciPy, scikit-learn, structural bioinformatics.

Evidence-Based Medicine Literature QA and Retraction-Risk Modelling

Medical NLP | Evidence Retrieval | Machine Learning

How can a medical answer show its sources, expose conflicting evidence, and make the limits of a risk prediction inspectable?

My contributionIndependently developed the literature-analysis tool and retraction-risk modelling workflow, spanning retrieval, evidence structuring, source traceability, model interpretation, and data-leakage auditing.

Goalfor clinical researchers and medical editors, build an evidence-analysis tool spanning medical-question retrieval, evidence grading, conclusion-conflict identification, and source traceability, together with a retraction-risk prediction model based on paper metadata.

Evidence retrieval and analysisbuilt a PubMed literature retrieval and question-answering pipeline on FastAPI using BM25 retrieval, PICO (population, intervention, comparator, outcome) question structuring, study-design identification, and five-level evidence grading; evidence disagreement is surfaced explicitly through support, oppose, and neutral conclusion classification with conflict detection, and conclusions in answers are traceable to specific PMIDs.

Retraction-risk predictionbuilt a retraction-risk dataset from 27,978 papers, modelled with LightGBM and interpreted with SHAP feature-contribution analysis; in the cross-validation experiments reported in the repository, LightGBM reached AUROC 0.856 and PR-AUC 0.812.

Data-quality and leakage auditidentified and handled publication-type label leakage and implicit leakage from cleared or replaced post-retraction abstracts, and assessed generalization through temporal extrapolation validation split by publication year; SHAP analysis revealed pronounced temporal censoring bias in publication year, avoiding mistaking superficial predictive performance for a genuine risk signal.

Engineering and validationimplemented online PubMed retrieval, offline corpus fallback, TTL caching, and flagging of retracted, questioned, or corrected literature; the project includes 30 unit tests, with repository-reported cache-hit latency of about 6 ms.

From question to evidencePICO structuring separates the population, intervention, comparator, and outcome before evidence is reviewed. Retrieved studies are organized by design and evidence level, and their conclusions are classified as supporting, opposing, or neutral. PMID links let readers return to the underlying paper and inspect disagreements rather than relying on a single unqualified answer.

Interpreting retraction riskThe metadata model is a separate analytical component from the question-answering pipeline. SHAP helps inspect which features contribute to its predictions. Leakage checks and publication-year splits address whether performance reflects information available at prediction time, rather than signals introduced after retraction. The reported cross-validation scores should be read alongside those audits, not as proof of prospective reliability.

Tech stackPython, FastAPI, BM25, PICO, LightGBM, SHAP, PubMed API.

PRRX2–SPP1 crosstalk in gemcitabine resistance

Single-cell RNA-seq | Spatial Transcriptomics | Tumor Microenvironment

How do cancer cells and macrophages cooperate during chemotherapy resistance?

Research questionIdentify cellular interactions associated with gemcitabine resistance in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma.

ApproachCombined bulk, single-cell, and spatial transcriptomics with trajectory and communication analyses; validation included cell-line experiments and clinical multicolor immunofluorescence.

Key findingsResistant samples were enriched for PRRX2-positive epithelial cancer cells and SPP1-positive macrophages. Their TGFB1/TGFBR2 signaling was linked to epithelial–mesenchymal transition and matrix remodeling. PRRX2 promoted TGFB1 and FN1 programs; reducing PRRX2 decreased cancer-cell migration.

Implications and limitsThe combined cellular signature correlated with worse prognosis. Dasatinib and ADC targets TROP2, MUC1, and CEACAM5 emerged as potential treatment directions, requiring further validation.

Repetitive cell-free DNA for plasma cancer detection

Liquid Biopsy | Fragmentomics | Machine Learning

Can repetitive DNA fragments improve low-depth blood-based cancer detection?

Research questionEvaluate whether repetitive-element fragmentation in plasma can distinguish cancer from healthy samples and help identify tumor origin.

ApproachProfiled 112 patients across five cancer types and 32 healthy volunteers using low-pass whole-genome sequencing. The model combined fragment ratio, length, distribution, complexity, and expansion.

Key findingsAlu elements and short tandem repeats were particularly informative. At 0.1× coverage, the study reported a detection AUC of 0.9824 in its validation cohort. Regulatory-region fragment features also supported tissue-of-origin classification.

Implications and limitsThe approach suggests a lower-sequencing-cost route to multi-cancer detection. Performance comes from research cohorts; prospective screening studies and larger external cohorts are needed.

Expression heterogeneity and candidate combination therapies

Single-cell RNA-seq | Multi-omics | Drug Response

How do AURKA expression states reveal different tumor programs?

Research questionCharacterize AURKA heterogeneity in prostate adenocarcinoma and investigate opportunities for treatment stratification.

ApproachIntegrated TCGA and GEO multi-omics profiles with single-cell epithelial-cell analysis, clinical associations, drug-sensitivity predictions, and cell-line knockdown experiments.

Key findingsAURKA-high epithelial subsets showed cell-cycle and DNA-repair programs, while lower-expression subsets favored TNF-α and androgen-response pathways. Silencing AURKA reduced colony formation, migration, and invasion in prostate cancer cells. The findings support a possible compensatory role after androgen-receptor inhibition.

Implications and limitsMolecular stratification suggested combinations of AURKA inhibitors with targeted drugs, antibody–drug conjugates, or immunotherapy. These combinations were proposed from computational evidence and require experimental and clinical confirmation.

PRRX2-driven fibroblasts and perineural invasion

Single-cell RNA-seq | Stromal Biology | Immunotherapy

How do fibroblast programs connect neural invasion with treatment outcome?

Research questionIdentify fibroblast states and regulatory mechanisms associated with perineural invasion in colorectal cancer.

ApproachIntegrated 20 single-cell datasets comprising 575,768 cells, mapped bulk invasion phenotypes with Scissor, and used deconvolution, regulatory-network analysis, and PRRX2 perturbation experiments.

Key findingsMMP2-positive myofibroblasts were enriched in invasion-positive tumors. PRRX2 linked this population to TGF-β signaling and extracellular matrix remodeling. PRRX2 knockdown reduced fibroblast proliferation, invasion, and TGFB1/NGF expression. Higher PRRX2 scores were associated with advanced disease and worse prognosis.

Implications and limitsThe score also showed associations with checkpoint-inhibitor outcome. Those treatment-response analyses used gastric and renal cancer cohorts, so prospective validation in colorectal cancer remains necessary. Suggested alternative therapies are exploratory.

Genomic landscape of Chinese metastatic hormone-sensitive disease

Targeted Sequencing | Population Genomics | Precision Oncology

Which genomic alterations characterize a Chinese mHSPC cohort?

Research questionDescribe genomic alterations in Chinese patients with metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer and their clinical associations.

ApproachRetrospectively analyzed targeted tumor sequencing from 52 patients and compared mutation patterns with the SU2C-PCF reference cohort.

Key findingsFOXA1 alterations occurred in 36.54% of the cohort; homologous-recombination repair gene alterations were also detected in 36.54%. FOXA1, KDM6A, PIK3CA, and CTNNB1 mutations were more frequent than in the reference cohort, while AR mutations were less frequent. TP53 alterations were enriched in high-Gleason-score tumors.

Implications and limitsThe results motivate genomic profiling and further investigation of repair-pathway alterations. The small retrospective cohort and differences in disease state between comparison cohorts limit conclusions about ancestry or treatment benefit.

Integrating inflammation and immunogenicity in clear cell renal cancer

Multi-omics | Machine Learning | Immune Checkpoint Blockade

Can combined immune and inflammatory features improve response prediction?

Research questionPredict checkpoint-blockade response and survival in clear cell renal cell carcinoma by combining inflammatory and immunogenic signals.

ApproachDerived inflammatory signatures from bulk and single-cell data covering more than 1,900 autoimmune-nephropathy patients, then integrated renal-cancer genomic and transcriptomic features. The study evaluated its TIs-ML model in more than 1,000 immunotherapy-treated patients.

Key findingsThe analysis identified 716 inflammation-related genes and two subtypes with different outcomes. Combining gene, pathway, immune-cell, mutation, co-mutation, and mutational-burden features improved reported response and survival prediction compared with individual biomarkers.

Implications and limitsThe work supports evaluating multiple aspects of tumor–immune biology together. Reported performance reflects the analyzed datasets; prospective validation is needed before using the model to guide treatment decisions.
